# LinkedIn: The Lara Acosta Method

A system for expert-led LinkedIn content that builds authority and generates inbound — not vanity likes.

**Core thesis:** You don't win by being a better writer. You win by being a *clear expert who can communicate*. Growth = expert positioning (SMPV) + intentional content (SLAY) + daily distribution + a simple conversion path.

## Step 0 — Get positioning before writing anything

Never write a post for someone whose SMPV you don't know. If it's missing from the conversation, ask for it in ONE short message (max 4 questions):

**SMPV = Skill → Market → Problem → Value**
- **Skill** — what you actually do well
- **Market** — exactly who you serve
- **Problem** — the expensive problem they have
- **Value** — how your content proves you can solve it

*Lara's own:* Skill: build online businesses through content · Market: founders and business owners · Problem: gaining authority organically and monetising it · Value: step-by-step content showing how.

If the user is impatient, infer SMPV from their bio/draft, state your assumption in one line, and write. Never stall the deliverable behind an interview.

Then lock **3 content pillars** (not 5, not random). Topic consistency is non-negotiable — no jumping food → family → business. Typical split: ~45% craft/mechanics of your niche, ~30% founder/practitioner stories, ~20% opinion & mindset reframes, ~5% offer/proof.

## Step 1 — Pick the post type

| Goal | Type | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Teach + build trust (default) | SLAY story-lesson | Story → Lesson → Actionable → You |
| Prove expertise fast | Specificity-stack teach post | Numbers → what nobody told me → 5 tactics |
| Stand out / start debate | Counter-intuitive reframe | Common advice → "I disagree" → what works |
| Convert | EduSales post | Teach the method → soft proof → permission-to-sell CTA |
| Humanise / borrow trust | Halo-effect post | Event, collab, win, small milestone |

## Step 2 — Write with SLAY

- **S — Story (3–5 lines).** Concrete, specific, with tension. A moment, not a topic. The hook lives here.
- **L — Lesson (1 sentence).** The insight the story earns. Skipping this is the #1 mistake — without it you wrote a diary entry.
- **A — Actionable (3–5 steps).** Numbered. One sentence each. Implementable today.
- **Y — You.** A direct question narrow enough to answer in one sentence.

Target ~150–250 words. Arrive at the point fast, and finish with a line that summarises the whole post.

## Step 3 — The line-by-line hook rule

**Every line must be able to stand alone as a hook.** Readers skim; each line has one job — earn the next line.

Practical rules:
- One idea per line. One line per paragraph. Blank line between each.
- First 2 lines must survive truncation (~140–210 chars before "…see more").
- No line may be scaffolding ("So here's the thing", "Let me explain", "Long story short").
- Broad → narrow arc: a universally relatable opener, then a personal story, then a niche-specific lesson. Wide reach, right reader.
- Use `→` or `>` for sub-points instead of bullet characters.
- Bold sparingly — roughly one phrase every 3–4 lines, on the beats a skimmer must catch.
- No links in the post body; put them in the first comment.
- 0–3 hashtags max, or none.

## Step 4 — Self-audit before delivering

Run every draft through this. Fix, don't just flag.

1. Would line 1 stop a scroll on its own?
2. Is every line independently interesting? Delete or rewrite the weak ones.
3. Is there a specific number, name, timeframe, or outcome? Specificity *is* credibility.
4. Is the lesson one clear sentence?
5. Can a reader do something today?
6. Can the CTA be answered in one sentence?
7. Does it tie back to one of the 3 pillars and the SMPV market?
8. Read it aloud — does it sound like this person, or like LinkedIn?
9. Any em-dash-heavy, "In today's fast-paced", "Let's dive in", tricolon-heavy AI residue? Strip it.

Deliver the post as plain text ready to paste — no markdown headers, no `**bold**` syntax (tell the user to use a Unicode bold formatter if they want bold).

## Step 5 — Distribution (content is half the job)

- **Post 3–5×/week.** Solo operator with no system: start at 3. Tue/Wed tend to perform best.
- **Engage 30–60 min/day.** Thoughtful comments that start discussions, on your ICP's and larger creators' posts — before and after you publish. LinkedIn now rewards posts that sustain long discussions, so old posts can resurface.
- Reply to every comment on your own post, in full sentences that add a new idea.
- Add connections daily, intentionally, inside your market.
- **Weekly:** one positioning/authority post, one media or format-different post, one celebration/proof post.
- **Monthly:** halo-effect content — events, collabs, big-name proximity, milestones. Small wins count (first client, first talk, first 100 followers). It makes you human *and* credible.

## Step 6 — Conversion (EduSales)

Attention → lead magnet → email list → offer → ascension. Teach the *what* and the *why* publicly; sell the *done-with-you*. Ask permission to sell in DMs instead of pitching cold. If content built trust, selling is frictionless.

Cadence, funnel design, lead-magnet patterns, DM scripts: `references/distribution.md`

## Rules of the method

- Format-chasing is a trap. There's no magic format; text-only can beat carousels. Chase clarity.
- Specificity over polish: "0 to 107K in 18 months" beats "grew significantly".
- Don't fabricate proof. If the user has no revenue story, use client outcomes, time saved, before/after, or qualitative wins.
- Don't clone Lara's voice ("babe", "queen", "literally") unless it's genuinely the user's register — imitation reads as cosplay and costs credibility.
- Write in the user's own vocabulary. Ask for 2–3 of their past posts when voice matters and mirror sentence length, punctuation habits, and slang.
- Never invent metrics, clients, or stories on the user's behalf. Ask, or leave `[bracketed placeholders]`.
