# Hooks

The hook is the first 1–2 lines — everything visible before "…see more" (~140–210 characters on mobile). But the discipline goes further: **every line in the post is a hook for the line below it.**

## The 5 formulas

### 1. Curiosity drop
Signals you have something specific without revealing it.
- "I've never talked about this publicly before."
- "I'm sharing something I usually keep behind a paywall."
- "Here's a [niche] strategy almost nobody is using yet."

Works because: information gap + implied exclusivity. Scrolling past feels like losing something.

### 2. Specificity stack
Numbers, timeframes, credentials — instant authority.
- "In 18 months I went from 0 to 107K followers."
- "I've audited 500+ [things] this year."
- "6 months. 40 videos. Here's what actually moved the needle."

Works because: specific numbers read as true and pre-qualify the promise.

### 3. Expectation break
A flat statement that violates a professional norm.
- "I hired him without an interview."
- "I fired my biggest client last week."
- "I stopped replying to DMs. Revenue went up."

Works because: the brain registers "that's not how you're supposed to do that" and needs the resolution.

### 4. Actionable promise
Names exactly what the reader will be able to do.
- "Read this and you'll never write a bad hook again."
- "This 5-minute exercise will rewrite your headline."
- "Steal this to get your first paying client."

Works because: tangible, time-bounded payoff lowers the cost of reading.

### 5. Counter-intuitive reframe
Inverts standard advice; the hook itself pays off.
- "Stop trying to go viral. Go specific."
- "Posting daily is the worst LinkedIn advice."
- "Your niche isn't too small. Your positioning is too vague."

Works because: the inversion delivers value immediately, and the post justifies it.

## Swipe file (adapt, don't paste)

**Story openers**
- "[Number] months ago I [decision most people wouldn't make]."
- "The email said: '[short quote].' I read it three times."
- "I almost didn't [do the thing]. Here's what I'd have missed."
- "My [client/boss/mentor] said one sentence that changed how I [do X]."
- "I was [X] years old and [specific humbling detail]."

**Authority openers**
- "I've [done thing] [N] times. [N-1] of them failed."
- "[N] years in [field] taught me [number] things. Only [smaller number] mattered."
- "Last quarter we [specific measurable result]. Here's the system."
- "I've reviewed [N] [artifacts]. The same [N] mistakes show up every time."

**Contrarian openers**
- "Unpopular: [common practice] is [wasting X]."
- "[Widely praised thing] is the most overrated advice in [field]."
- "Everyone's optimising [X]. Almost nobody is fixing [Y]."
- "You don't have a [surface problem] problem. You have a [root] problem."

**Problem-agitate openers**
- "You're [doing the right activity] and still [not getting the result]."
- "If your [metric] is [state], it's not [assumed cause]. It's this:"
- "[Audience]: this is why [painful outcome] keeps happening."

**Teaching openers**
- "How to [outcome] without [the thing they dread]:"
- "The [N]-step system I use to [outcome] in [timeframe]:"
- "Steal my [artifact]. It took [time] to build."

**Vulnerability openers**
- "I made [mistake] and it cost me [specific amount/thing]."
- "For [duration] I believed [wrong belief]. It stalled me."
- "The hardest part of [thing] nobody warns you about:"

## Rewriting a weak hook

Given a draft opener, run this drill:

1. **Cut the wind-up.** Delete everything before the first interesting word. Most hooks start one or two sentences too early.
2. **Add one specific.** A number, a name, a timeframe, a dollar figure, a place.
3. **Remove the conclusion.** If the hook already tells them the answer, there's nothing to click for.
4. **Shorten to ≤ 12 words** if possible. Then check it still makes sense alone.
5. **Test the second line.** It must add tension, not restate line 1.
6. **Say it out loud.** If you wouldn't say it to a colleague, rewrite it.

**Before:** "I wanted to share some thoughts on the importance of pricing your services correctly, which is something a lot of freelancers struggle with."
**After:** "A client told me my pricing was insulting. I doubled it the next day."

## Hook anti-patterns

- Questions as openers ("Ever wondered if…?") — low commitment, easy to scroll past.
- "Thrilled to announce" / "Humbled to share".
- Emoji-led first lines.
- Vague superlatives with no proof ("game-changing insights").
- Fake cliffhangers the post never resolves — burns trust, kills the next post's reach.
- Engagement bait ("comment YES if you agree") with no substance behind it.
