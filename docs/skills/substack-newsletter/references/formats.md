# Format Anatomies

Section-by-section blueprints with word budgets. Budgets are proportional guides, not quotas — if the idea runs short, ship it short.

---

## 1. Analytical deep dive (1,500–3,000)

*One event, one reframe. Stratechery / The Diff lineage.*

| Section | Words | Job |
|---|---|---|
| News peg | 100–200 | What happened, flatly. Link it. No drama. |
| The consensus read | 200–300 | What everyone thinks it means, steelmanned |
| The reframe | 600–1,200 | Why that's incomplete; the structural argument |
| Evidence | 300–600 | Numbers, precedent, mechanism |
| Implications | 300–500 | What follows if you're right |
| Close | 50–100 | The sentence they'll quote |

**Failure modes:** summarizing the news instead of arguing about it; hedging the reframe into mush; forgetting to say what it means for the reader.

---

## 2. Narrative deep dive (3,000–8,000)

*Not Boring / The Generalist lineage. A story with a thesis.*

| Section | Words | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | 150–400 | Scene, anecdote, or surprising claim |
| The promise | 100–200 | What you'll understand by the end |
| Contents | — | Optional; include above ~4,000 words |
| Origins | 400–800 | How it started, why it existed |
| Mechanics | 800–2,000 | How the thing actually works, from primitives |
| The market | 500–1,200 | Competition, dynamics, map |
| The thesis | 500–1,000 | Your argument, committed |
| The bear case | 300–600 | Strongest counterargument, honestly |
| What to watch | 200–400 | Falsifiable signposts |
| Close | 100–200 | Zoom out |

**Pacing rules:** vary section length deliberately. Insert a chart, image, or pull quote roughly every 800 words. Never run more than three consecutive paragraphs of the same length — monotony, not length, is what loses readers.

**Failure modes:** research dump with no thesis; thesis with no evidence; uniform pacing.

---

## 3. Wry running commentary (2,000–4,000, multi-item)

*Money Stuff lineage. High frequency only — three-plus sends a week.*

Structure: 3–6 independent items, each 300–800 words, each with its own subhead. Ordered by interest, not importance. Optional recurring closing section.

**Per item:** scene (1–2 sentences) → the odd fact → the mechanism, explained clearly → the general principle → dry landing.

**Requirements:** a genuinely distinctive voice, a beat with steady small absurdities, and enough frequency that any single weak item is forgotten by tomorrow.

**Failure modes:** running it weekly (reads as having no thesis); explaining the joke; items with no mechanism, only summary.

---

## 4. Practical operator post (1,200–2,500)

*Lenny lineage. Answers a job-shaped question.*

| Section | Words | Job |
|---|---|---|
| The question | 50–150 | Verbatim from a reader if possible |
| Why it's hard | 150–300 | Why the obvious answer fails |
| The framework | 600–1,200 | Numbered steps or decision tree |
| Practitioner evidence | 300–600 | Named people, real examples |
| The artifact | — | Template, checklist, benchmark table |
| Caveats | 150–300 | When this doesn't apply |
| Next action | 50–100 | One thing to do this week |

**Failure modes:** generic advice with no named sources; a framework with no artifact; no caveats (reads as overclaiming).

---

## 5. Personal essay (1,200–2,500)

*Culture Study lineage.*

| Section | Words | Job |
|---|---|---|
| The specific observation | 200–400 | Small, concrete, often mundane |
| The generalization | 150–300 | It's not just me — here's the pattern |
| The analysis | 500–1,000 | Structural, economic, historical causes |
| Complication | 200–400 | What's messy; where your framing fails |
| Open question | 50–150 | Genuine, answerable, drives comments |

**Failure modes:** anecdote with no analysis (diary, not essay); analysis with no specific (lecture); a closing question so broad nobody can answer it.

---

## 6. Contextual dispatch (600–1,200)

*Letters from an American lineage. High cadence, single lens.*

Event, stated plainly (150) → the lens applied — history, data, domain expertise (400–700) → what it reveals that coverage missed (200–300) → where the thread goes (100).

**Requirements:** genuine expertise and the ability to sustain very high cadence. Plain prose; no formatting flourishes.

**Failure modes:** applying the lens loosely so it becomes generic commentary; missing sends.

---

## 7. Curated digest (500–1,200)

*Morning Brew lineage.*

Opener with personality (75–150) → 4–8 items ordered by importance → skimmable close.

**Per item (75–200 words):** punchy headline → what happened, 2–3 sentences → **why it matters**, 1–2 sentences → optional wry aside.

The "why it matters" line is non-negotiable — it's the entire difference between a newsletter and an RSS feed.

**Failure modes:** links without interpretation; too many items (cap at 8); inconsistent voice.

---

## 8. Teardown (2,000–4,000)

*Growth In Reverse lineage. Best entry format for a new publication.*

| Section | Words | Job |
|---|---|---|
| The result | 100–200 | The number that earns the read |
| The subject | 200–300 | Who, and why they're worth studying |
| Timeline | 600–1,200 | Chronological, inflection points marked |
| The mechanisms | 800–1,500 | Each tactic named and explained |
| Replicable vs. not | 300–500 | Honest separation, including unfair advantages |
| Your action items | 200–400 | Specific, this-week |

**Failure modes:** admiring narrative with no extractable mechanism; pretending everything is replicable.

---

## 9. Modular short (300–800)

*3-2-1 lineage. Fixed container, every time.*

Pick a shape and never change it. Examples: three ideas / two quotes / one question. One thing I learned / one thing I'm watching / one thing I'm asking. Three links with a paragraph each.

**Rules:** identical structure every issue; each module self-contained and independently shareable; ruthless compression — every sentence earns its place.

**Failure modes:** changing the format (destroys the recognizability that is the entire point); modules that only make sense in sequence.

---

## Choosing under constraints

- **Under 3 hours a week:** modular short, curated digest, or contextual dispatch.
- **Not yet an authority:** teardown or practical operator post — both build authority through rigor rather than requiring it.
- **Strong voice, dry subject:** wry commentary or narrative deep dive.
- **B2B audience with budget:** practical operator post — highest paid conversion of any format.
- **Broad consumer audience:** contextual dispatch or personal essay — highest volume ceiling.

## Mixing formats

One primary, at most one secondary, on a fixed day. Tuesday deep dive plus Friday links is a publication. Three rotating formats is a blog, and a blog doesn't build a habit.
