# Welcome Sequence

Five emails. The highest-engagement window you will ever have with a subscriber — most publications waste it on a single automated "thanks for subscribing."

Goals, in order: deliver the promised thing immediately, prove quality, become a person, get a reply, present the offer.

---

## Email 1 — Immediately

**Subject:** `Here's [the thing]` or `You're in`

- Deliver the lead magnet in the first line. No preamble.
- One paragraph: what this newsletter is and who it's for.
- What to expect: format, day, frequency. Be specific — "Tuesdays, about 1,200 words on X."
- Ask them to reply with what brought them here. This single ask does more for deliverability than any technical fix.
- Tell them to whitelist / move to Primary.

Keep it under 200 words.

---

## Email 2 — Day 2

**Subject:** the actual subject line of your best-ever post

- Send your single best piece. Not a summary of it — the piece.
- One line of framing: "This is the one people send to their team most often. It's a good sense of what you're in for."
- No other asks.

---

## Email 3 — Day 4

**Subject:** `Who I am and why I write this`

- The short version of your story — specifically the part that gives you the right to write this newsletter.
- What you believe about this space that most people in it don't.
- What you're working on now.
- Invite a reply: "What are you working on?"

This is the email that converts subscribers into readers. People stay for a person.

---

## Email 4 — Day 7

**Subject:** `A question for you`

- One genuine question, easy to answer in a sentence.
- Explain that you actually read and reply to these.
- Optionally: two or three archive links grouped by pillar, so they can go deeper on what matters to them.

Replies are the strongest deliverability signal available and the best source of future post ideas.

---

## Email 5 — Day 10–14

**Subject:** varies — the offer

- Only if you have a paid tier, community, or product.
- What it is, what's included, what it costs, who it's for and who it isn't.
- Honest framing: what the paid tier lets you do that you otherwise couldn't.
- Founding or new-subscriber offer if you have one.
- No guilt, no urgency theater. Ask once, clearly, and move on.

If you don't have an offer yet, replace this with a curated "best of the archive" send.

---

## Notes

**Adapt to source.** Subscribers from recommendations or cross-promos are lower-intent than direct signups. A shorter, harder-working sequence for those sources is worth building once you can segment.

**Then merge them into the regular schedule.** Don't run a permanent parallel track.

**Review it every quarter.** The welcome sequence is the most-read thing you'll ever write and the least-updated. Most writers set it up once and never look again.
