# Platforms & Distribution

---

## Own domain vs. Medium

Not a binary. They do different jobs.

| | Own domain | Medium |
|---|---|---|
| **Audience on day one** | Zero | Built-in, algorithmic |
| **SEO equity** | Yours, compounds forever | Medium's |
| **Email capture** | Unrestricted | Limited |
| **Design & structure** | Full control, can build clusters | Fixed |
| **Monetization** | Anything | Partner Program + off-platform |
| **Risk** | You bear all of it | Algorithm and policy can change overnight |
| **Time to first reader** | Months | Days |
| **Best for** | Long-term asset, clusters, business | Fast reach, testing, authority signals |

**The honest read:** a new site has no audience and no authority, so publishing exclusively there means writing into silence for months. Medium solves the cold-start problem and solves nothing about ownership. The answer for most people is both.

---

## The hybrid pattern

The standard approach that captures both:

1. **Publish canonical on your own domain first.** Let it get indexed — wait a few days at minimum.
2. **Syndicate to Medium** using Medium's import tool, which sets `rel="canonical"` back to your original automatically.
3. Do the same for LinkedIn articles, dev.to, Hashnode, or any relevant community platform.

**Why the order matters.** Publishing natively on Medium first means Medium owns the SEO equity for that piece, and your own version becomes the duplicate. `rel=canonical` tells search engines which version is authoritative and prevents duplicate-content dilution — but only if it points the right way.

**What syndication gets you:** platform audience, referral traffic, backlinks to the canonical, and social proof. What it doesn't get you: rankings for the syndicated copy. That's the trade, and it's usually worth it.

## Medium specifics

**Curation is the whole game.** Curated pieces reach dramatically more readers than uncurated ones — commonly reported at 10–100×. What curators reportedly look for: depth, originality, clear structure, proper formatting, concrete examples, and genuine insight. What gets passed over: clickbait, generic advice, thin listicles, and promotional content.

**Publications provide instant distribution.** Submitting to an established publication in your niche puts you in front of its subscriber base rather than starting from your own follower count. This is the single biggest lever available to a new Medium writer.

**Earnings follow reading time, not views.** The Partner Program pays based on how long paying members spend reading, weighted by engagement. Practical consequences:
- An article with 11 reads can out-earn one with 17, if the readers were members who finished it.
- Depth and completion matter more than clicks. Clickbait that people bounce off earns nothing.
- Member versus non-member reads matter, so writing for the kind of person who pays for Medium matters.

**Cadence.** Algorithmic feeds reward recent activity, so frequency matters more here than on your own site. Consistent publishing of 2–3 pieces weekly is a commonly cited baseline for meaningful follower growth; the highest-volume operators historically ran 4–5 weekly. This is the opposite of the search calculus, where accumulated authority matters more than recency.

**Engagement is part of the algorithm.** Reply to comments. Read and comment substantively on others in your niche. Visibility on the platform is partly a function of participation in it.

**One or two topics.** Recognizability compounds. Readers should know what they get from your name.

**Always redirect off-platform.** Every piece should point somewhere you own — a newsletter, a lead magnet, a site. Medium's rules, algorithm, and payout formula have all changed repeatedly and will again. Treat it as rented distribution, not a home.

## Other platforms worth considering

**LinkedIn articles and posts.** Excellent for B2B. Native posts massively outperform link posts in reach. Rewrite for the platform rather than pasting; syndicate full articles only after canonical publication.

**dev.to / Hashnode** for technical content — both support canonical URLs cleanly and have engaged, genuinely useful audiences.

**Reddit and niche forums.** Heavily cited by AI answer engines, which makes genuine participation a legitimate GEO play. But: be useful for weeks before you ever mention your own work, and even then mention it once. Self-promotional posting gets removed and damages the account.

**YouTube.** Increasingly relevant to GEO — answer engines parse transcripts, so a well-structured video can earn a citation your text doesn't. Chapters, descriptive titles, and clean transcripts matter.

**Substack** for the email side. See the newsletter workflow if that's the goal; it complements a blog rather than replacing it.

## Distribution: the part everyone skips

**Publish-and-wait is the default failure mode.** Budget promotion as roughly half the work of each piece.

**For every post:**

1. **Email your list.** Highest-converting channel you have, by a wide margin.
2. **Atomize for social.** One article becomes 5–10 native posts across platforms. Rewrite for each; don't cross-post.
3. **Tell everyone you cited or quoted.** People share things they're in. This is the easiest link acquisition that exists.
4. **Post where the reader already is** — the specific subreddit, Slack, Discord, or forum. Once, genuinely, with context.
5. **Syndicate** to Medium / LinkedIn / dev.to with canonical set.
6. **Internal-link from your existing pages.** Immediately. This is free and most people forget.
7. **Resurface it in 3 months.** Content has a longer useful life than a single promotion cycle.

**For pieces built as linkable assets** (original research, tools, definitive guides), add:
- Direct outreach to people who've cited similar work — but personalized, referencing something specific. Templated skyscraper outreach now converts at roughly 1–3%.
- Pitch to relevant newsletters as a link or guest piece
- Submit to industry roundups and aggregators
- Offer the underlying dataset to journalists covering the space

## What to build on which platform

| Goal | Primary | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Long-term organic asset | Own domain, clusters | Syndicate to Medium |
| Fast audience from zero | Medium + publications | Own domain in parallel |
| B2B leads | Own domain + LinkedIn | Newsletter |
| Technical authority | Own domain + dev.to | YouTube |
| AI citation | Own domain + Reddit participation | Original research |
| Email list growth | Own domain + lead magnets | Every platform, pointing back |

The one constant: **something you own must be the destination.** Platforms are how people find you. They are not where you keep what you've built.
